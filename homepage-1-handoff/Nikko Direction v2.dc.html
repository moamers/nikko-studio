Plate 01 — Visual grammar

Five rules, no exceptions

R1

Cobalt carries the structure. Coral only punctuates. Cream is the room. Nothing shouts twice on one screen.

R2

Cut, arched or oval — never merely rounded. Radius is 0 or 50%; nothing in between, and no 999px capsules. Structure stays square; a container takes one clipped corner (dialect A) or an arched top (dialect C); small interactive things — buttons, chips, tags, avatars — are true ovals. Imagery is always torn and taped.

R3

Everything is catalogued. Every block wears a mono label: plate number, serial, date, status. Metadata is functional, never novelty.

R4

Brutal scale contrast. 11px mono sits in the same eyeline as 110px grotesk. The gap between them is the voice.

R5

Off register once per section. One thing tilted, taped or misprinted — never two. Asymmetry over a grid you can still feel.

Plate 04C — The breathing ground

The page changes temperature

The site's ground is not one flat cream. It drifts, over about a minute, through the palette at the faintest strength the screen can hold — paper, horizon, last light, offshore, and back. Nobody watching should be able to catch it moving; they should only notice, on the second scroll, that the room feels different. Same instinct as the logo's swapping line: one system, changing light.

Live on this page — the ground behind these plates is the animation. Drift and cycle length are both in the tweak panel under Ground.

Timing

48s full cycle, five stops, ease-in-out between each — roughly 9.6s per leg. Never linear: the ease is what stops it reading as a slideshow of colours.

Legal range is 30–90s. Under 30 it becomes an effect people watch; over 90 it stops being perceptible at all and you may as well pick one cream.

The loop must return to paper, and adjacent stops must be within about 4% lightness of each other. That is the whole trick — the value stays flat, only the hue rotates.

Colour derivation

Each stop is the brand colour mixed into paper at about 6–9%, then lifted back halfway to paper again. No new hues are ever introduced — the ground can only be tinted with horizon, last light or offshore.

Green, purple and any hue outside the palette are banned here, however subtle. A ground the audience cannot name as one of three colours is just a stale screen.

What must not move

Only the page ground drifts. Cards, plates, the memory box and every image mount stay fixed paper — if the containers drift too, the whole screen breathes and it reads as a broken monitor.

Ink, coral, cobalt and yellow never drift. Text contrast is measured against the darkest stop and must clear it at every point in the cycle.

prefers-reduced-motion holds the ground at paper, permanently. It is an atmosphere, never information.

Plate 04D — Turquoise, and where the Levantine touches the picture

Cut the photograph, not the interface

Turquoise is barred from the UI and the pattern is barred from behind body copy — which leaves one place they are both allowed to be loud: the imagery. Three mounts, using the archive we already have. The rule underneath all of them is that the Levantine shape is a window cut into the photo, never a sticker laid on top of it.

01 — Lantern cut

The mirror shape from the wall behind her, used as the mount for the picture of her in front of it. Turquoise sits underneath as the plaster, so the colour arrives as a wall, not as a brand tint. One cut per page.

02 — Inlay keyline

Ink frame, turquoise inlay line, a counted tatreez band along the foot like the stitched edge of a keepsake box. The band is the only tinted element and it never exceeds the width of the picture.

03 — Arched window

The house dome used as an aperture, with the scalloped foot picked out in turquoise — the same band the memory box wears, borrowed by the picture. Best on hero and case-study openers.

The turquoise licence

Turquoise may be a surface inside a picture — plaster, tile, thread, a mount. It may never be a fill, a rule, a chip, a link or a word. One turquoise element per page, always touching an image; the moment it appears next to type it has become a sixth accent and the hierarchy is gone. The Levantine shape follows the same licence: cut into the photograph, never applied over the interface.

Non-negotiable rules

  • The page lands silent. No autoplay with sound — browsers block it, and unannounced audio is an accessibility failure, not a surprise.
  • Invitation is visible within the first screen and styled as a cassette label, not a media widget.
  • Any clip over three seconds needs pause, stop and volume within reach at all times.
  • Transcript is on the page as text — for people who can't hear it and for the search engines that can't either.
  • One voice note per visit. Declined once, it stays declined for that session.
  • prefers-reduced-motion freezes the signal filter; the still image underneath still reads.

Plate 08G — De-boxing

Oval, and in colour

Square, capsule and the coloured fills are archived. What is left to decide is where the colour goes. The button law

One filled oval, one outlined oval, per page. The filled oval is ink, and the slot inside it carries the accent — horizon by default, offshore or last light where the page wants a different temperature. Two accents never share one button. On ink grounds the fill inverts to paper with a coral slot (D). The slot appears on the filled oval and nowhere else — never on cream, never on the outline, never on a chip.

A full oval container wastes its corners and fights every paragraph in it. The answer is a shallow dome — a lid, not an archway — with 8px on the bottom corners so the whole thing reads as an object rather than a doorway. This is the curve used everywhere on the site now.

Inputs stay boxes

Send it

Square-ish, 3px corner, 2px ink, focus turns the border cobalt. The oval belongs to actions — if the fields curve too, nothing signals which element does something. Subtle everywhere, edgy in one place per screen.

The oval law

Small and interactive → true oval (50%). Buttons, chips, tags, avatars, the play control. Nothing wider than about 5 words, so the ellipse stays an ellipse and never stretches into a capsule.

Large and content-bearing → shallow dome (50% 50% 8px 8px / 24% 24% 8px 8px). Cards, the memory box, section heads, image mounts. A lid, not an archway.

Structure and inputs → square. Plates, rules, tables, the grid, every field. And never 999px anywhere: the capsule is banned, that is what stops this looking like everyone else.
